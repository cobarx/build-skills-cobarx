# Do Deming's successors carry points 7–12?

Working notes, 2026-10-05. Not settled, and not expected to be in one pass. Raised in review of
#110: if the methodologies that followed TQM kept its measurement but not Deming's recommendations
for how an organisation is run, they dropped a key part of his work. This asks whether Lean, Six
Sigma and ISO 9000/9001 carry those recommendations.

The points that matter most here, in Hampton's judgement, are 9, 10, 11b, 12a and 12b. Points 7, 8
and 11a are covered briefly.

## Request and mechanism

Each point makes a request, and most also name a mechanism: an obstacle Deming observed in the
management of his time, to be removed. The mechanism is not the whole of the request. Removing it
may not be enough, and other actions may be needed. 12b says so itself: "This means, inter alia,
abolishment of the annual or merit rating and of management by objective." So each methodology is
judged here on whether it meets the request. Whether it keeps the obstacle Deming named is
evidence, not the test.

From the Deming Institute's condensation of *Out of the Crisis*, pp. 23–24
(<https://deming.org/explore/fourteen-points/>):

| Point | Request | Mechanism Deming named |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | People in research, design, sales and production "work as a team, to foresee problems of production and in use" | Break down barriers between departments |
| 10 | Stop holding the workforce to results whose causes "belong to the system and thus lie beyond the power of the work force" | Eliminate slogans, exhortations, and targets for zero defects and new levels of productivity |
| 11b | "Substitute leadership" for management by numbers | Eliminate management by objective, management by numbers, numerical goals |
| 12a | Hourly workers keep their "right to pride of workmanship"; supervisors are responsible for quality, not "sheer numbers" | Remove the barriers that rob them of it |
| 12b | The same right for people in management and engineering | Abolish, "inter alia", the annual or merit rating and management by objective |

Hampton's example of 12b's request, as distinct from its mechanism: when someone who is not a
product manager suggests a feature or challenges the product roadmap, how does the organisation
respond? A challenge dismissed because it comes from outside the owning role is a barrier to pride
of workmanship whether or not a merit rating exists. The response is also something an organisation
can be observed doing, which makes the request testable.

## Summary

On the requests, none of the three reliably carries the key points as a whole:

- **Lean** asks for most of them in its canon: teamwork across departments, workers improving their
  own jobs, leadership in place of management by numbers. It keeps two obstacles Deming named,
  cascaded numerical targets (hoshin kanri) and individual evaluation, and its advocates argue
  both serve learning rather than judgment. Whether the requests are met depends on the management:
  Lean's own leaders say most adoptions are shallow, with hoshin often "little more than rebranded
  management by objectives".
- **Six Sigma**'s statistical method meets the request in 10, since it puts variation on the
  system. The management programme around it, as GE and 3M ran it, works against 11b and 12b and
  goes beyond the obstacles Deming named: promotion and stock options gated on it, forced ranking
  with the bottom 10% removed "every year", and green-belt quotas.
- **ISO 9000/9001** states the requests in 9 and parts of 12 in its guidance on principles, but
  certification audits against ISO 9001's requirements, which do not ask for them. Its measurable
  objectives at "functions, levels and processes" are an obstacle only where they are applied to
  people. Deming called the standards "conformance specifications", "not enough". No study
  measures whether certification meets any of the requests.

A reading of the pattern, not a finding: the requests survive in each canon, and the instruments
management already uses (targets, appraisal, ranking) survive beside them, recast as compatible.
Practice evidence on the requests themselves is thin everywhere, and absent for pride of
workmanship.

## Lean and the Toyota Production System

| Point | Request met? | Obstacle Deming named, kept? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Canon asks for it: hoshin aligns "vertically and horizontally … across departments" (LEI) | Not applicable | Thin; a NUMMI ex-manager called GM a "throw it over the wall organization" |
| 10 | Canon puts abnormalities on the system: stop, fix, prevent recurrence (jidoka) | No evidence of slogans either way | None on exhortation found |
| 11b | Canon claims it: hoshin as direction, not appraisal | Kept: hoshin cascades numerical targets; Toyota's cascade "stops at the section manager level" (Smalley) | Liker and Shook: most efforts are "little more than rebranded management by objectives (MBO)"; Smalley names the failure mode, "hoshin becomes a personal-appraisal exercise" |
| 12a | Canon asks for it: frontline workers improve their own jobs (Spear & Bowen) | Fixed cycle times can act as quotas; the canon treats them as hypotheses to improve | NUMMI workers set their own standards and satisfaction rose; a review finds harm in low-complexity manual work |
| 12b | Not found in the canon for managers and engineers | Kept: Toyota evaluates individuals, on "ability to act and compassion"; whether that is the merit rating Deming meant is unknown | 2019: Toyota's union considered a five-grade evaluation for raises, which "reflects management's wishes" |

Other points: the canon asks for 7 (supervisors "as teachers") and 8 (any operator can stop the
line). NUMMI workers still reported "fear tactics", and a 2017 Toyota suicide was ruled
work-related harassment.

## Six Sigma

| Point | Request met? | Obstacle Deming named, kept? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Canon asks for cross-functional teams | Not applicable | GE's "boundaryless" idea predates Six Sigma; no study isolates Six Sigma's effect |
| 10 | The method meets it: common- versus special-cause variation puts defects on the system | Kept in form: Motorola's 3.4 defects-per-million target (checked only against Wikipedia), GE's goal to "raise the bar"; aimed at processes, not workers | Deming's *The New Economics* quotes Wheeler: "Zero Defects, and Six Sigma … all miss the point" (agent-checked) |
| 11b | Not as practised: ASQ's syllabus teaches SMART goals, OKRs and "a line of sight from performance measures to organizational strategies" | Kept | GE: executive pay "heavily weighted toward Six Sigma commitment and success" |
| 12a | Mostly silent; the variation model supports it | Not clearly | Two employee surveys found mild positives (abstracts only) |
| 12b | Worked against, at GE and 3M | Kept and extended: training "an ironclad prerequisite for promotion … and a requirement for any award of stock options" (GE 1997); forced ranking (GE 2000) | 3M researchers filled a "red book" of charts; Art Fry: "how fast a culture can be torn apart" |

The method and the programme separate cleanly in principle and rarely in practice. GE linked them
outright: its top 20% early-career performers were offered "a two-to-three-year Black Belt
assignment". The Deming Institute's writers deny the core is safe either: they argue a
specification-based focus is integral to Six Sigma (agent-checked via the Wayback Machine).

Other points: ASQ's syllabus covers "coaching multiple levels of leadership" (7), meaning deploying
projects. On 8, GE 1997 called it "unthinkable to hire into the Company, promote or tolerate those
who cannot, or will not, commit to this way of work". On 11a, 3M staff dreamed up green-belt
programmes "to fill their quota".

Key sources:

- GE annual reports, 1997 and 2000 (Welch's letters),
  <https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/NYSE_GE_1997.pdf> and
  <https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/NYSE_GE_2000.pdf>. The 2000
  letter: GE "must remove that lower 10%, and keep removing it every year".
- Hindo, "At 3M, a struggle between efficiency and creativity", *BusinessWeek*, 11 June 2007,
  <https://effectuation.org/hubfs/Journal%20Articles/2016/06/3m-struggle-between-efficiency-and-creativity.pdf>.
  It also reports cuts to capital spending, which confound any causal claim.
- ASQ, Six Sigma Black Belt Body of Knowledge, 2022,
  <https://www.asq.org/cert/resource/pdf/certification/2022-SSBB-BoK.pdf>. Nothing on fear,
  merit rating or pride of workmanship.

## ISO 9000 and ISO 9001

| Point | Request met? | Obstacle Deming named, kept? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Asked for in the principles ("Promote collaboration"; "reduced cross-functional barriers"), weakly in the requirements | Not applicable | None found |
| 10 | Not addressed | Contested: everyone must be aware of the quality policy and objectives (7.3), which critics read as a slogan | None found |
| 11b | Leadership is required (5.1.1) only toward the quality management system | Only where applied to people: objectives must "be measurable" at "relevant functions, levels and processes" (6.2) | None measured |
| 12a | Not addressed: "Recognize and acknowledge people's contribution" | Not applicable | None found |
| 12b | Not addressed | Leans toward it: the principles suggest "self-evaluation of performance against personal objectives" | None found |

Other points: the principles ask for 7 (leaders "create conditions in which people are engaged")
and 8 ("take initiatives without fear"); the requirements are silent on 8 and 11a.

Practice evidence is indirect. Levine & Toffel (*Management Science*, 2010) found adopters had
lower death rates and higher growth in sales, employment and earnings, but measured none of
Deming's requests, and say non-experimental data "cannot definitively attribute" the outcomes to
adoption. Boiral (*Organization Science*, 2003) found support for ISO 9000 "often … only
superficial", with "ceremonial integrators" among the responses (abstract only).

Key sources:

- ISO, *Quality management principles*, 2015,

  of people" it says "take initiatives without fear" and "self-evaluation of performance against

- Deming, interviewed by Tim Stevens, *IndustryWeek*, 17 January 1994: "ISO 9000, 9001, 9002 are

## Why the requests were dropped

Working framings, to be refined. Two that fit different cases.

**Decoupling.** Adopting a practice's form without its substance. Boiral's ISO 9000 interviews fit
it: support "often … only superficial", "ceremonial integrators" among the responses. The term
comes from institutional theory, Meyer and Rowan's "myth and ceremony" (1977); that attribution is
recalled, not checked.

**Enshittification, with employees as the locked-in users.** Hampton's framing. Doctorow's
enshittification is a platform turning on users it has locked in, to move value to itself. His
stages (good to users, then worse for users to favour business customers, then worse for both to
claw value back for the platform) are recalled here, not checked. Doctorow names the forces that
"once acted as constraints" as "competition, regulation, labor power and interoperability"
(Pluralistic, 8 May 2025, <https://pluralistic.net/2025/05/08/who-broke-the-internet/>). Read with
employees as the users:

- **Good to the users first.** At NUMMI, Toyota's management came with a no-layoff policy, and
  worker satisfaction rose from about 80% to over 90% (Adler, agent-checked).
- **Lock-in.** Skills worth most at one firm, and, in the US, benefits tied to the job. Reasoning,
  not a source.
- **Value moved to the owners.** GE 1997: senior executive pay "heavily weighted toward Six Sigma
  commitment and success — success now increasingly defined as 'eatable' financial returns". At
  3M, Six Sigma came with 8,000 layoffs and an intensified performance review.
- **The constraint removed.** Points 8 and 12 (no fear, pride of workmanship, no merit rating) give
  workers standing inside the firm: a labour constraint built into management. GE's 2000 policy to
  remove the bottom 10% "every year" is a fear instrument aimed at it.

Where it fits less well: at GE, forced ranking came with Six Sigma from the start, not after a
good phase; ISO looks more like ceremony than extraction, which is decoupling. And Doctorow coined
the term for platforms, so applying it to employers is an extension of it. This framing is the
bridge to the anti-enshittification goal raised in #90's notes, and is meant to be refined there.

**Scale and diffuse ownership.** Hampton's framing. As corporations grew larger and more beholden
to shareholders, scale and dispersed ownership added layers of abstraction between the people who
decide and the work, which interferes with breaking down barriers between departments (9). The same
pressure throws out points 1 (constancy of purpose) and 2 (adopt the new philosophy). Deming named
it in his deadly diseases: "Lack of constancy of purpose to plan product and service that will have
a market and keep the company in business, and provide jobs", and "Emphasis on short-term profits:
short-term thinking (just the opposite from constancy of purpose to stay in business), fed by fear
of unfriendly takeover, and by push from bankers and owners for dividends"
(<https://deming.org/explore/seven-deadly-diseases/>). To refine: whether scale itself is the
cause or the governance that comes with it. Toyota is very large, so a counterexample to scale
alone; how its ownership differs is not checked here.

## Deming's later emphasis

Hampton's reading: over time Deming came to see that psychology was supported by measurement, so
measurement stopped being the primary focus. Not a discounting of measurement; an ordering. The
record of the shift:

- 1950, to Japanese industrial leaders: "Statistical product quality administration is a splendid
  new tool"
  (<https://deming.org/speech-by-dr-deming-to-japanese-business-leaders-in-1950/>).
- *Out of the Crisis*, deadly disease 5: "Management by use only of visible figures, with
  little or no consideration of figures that are unknown or unknowable"
  (<https://deming.org/myth-if-you-cant-measure-it-you-cant-manage-it/>).
- 1989, the first presentation of what became the System of Profound Knowledge lists psychology
  and "knowledge of cooperation and its benefits" beside knowledge of variation
  (<https://deming.org/the-development-of-demings-management-system/>).
- Later, in *The New Economics*: "It is wrong to suppose that if you can't measure it, you can't
  manage it – a costly myth" (same Deming Institute page as deadly disease 5).

By that reading, a successor that makes measurement its primary focus inverts the order Deming
arrived at, whatever else it keeps.

## What would settle it

- **Studies that measure the requests directly.** None was found for any of the three: teamwork
  across functions, whether workers are held to system-caused results, how targets are used, pride
  in work, compared between adopters and non-adopters. This is the central gap.
- **A direct test of 12b.** How adopters respond when someone outside the owning role suggests a
  feature or challenges a plan: heard and weighed, or dismissed. Observable in any organisation,
  whichever methodology it uses.
- **Request separated from mechanism.** Organisations that keep an obstacle Deming named (an
  individual evaluation, a cascaded target) yet meet the request, and ones that remove it and still
  fail. Either would show how far the mechanism decides the outcome.
- **Method separated from programme.** Six Sigma deployments with and without forced ranking or
  bonus links, compared. The same for hoshin kanri used for direction versus for appraisal.
- **Toyota's own appraisal of managers and engineers,** and whether the 2019 five-grade proposal
  was adopted.
- **Primary texts not yet read:** Seddon's *In Pursuit of Quality: The Case Against ISO 9000*
  (1997), Harry & Schroeder's *Six Sigma* (2000), Parker 2003 in full, the ISO 9001:2015 standard.
