# Do Deming's successors carry points 7–12?

Working notes, 2026-10-05. Not settled, and not expected to be in one pass. Raised in review of
#110: if the methodologies that followed TQM kept its measurement but not Deming's recommendations
for how an organisation is run, they dropped a key part of his work. This asks whether Lean, Six
Sigma and ISO 9000/9001 call for points 7–12 (their canon), and whether they produce them as adopted
(practice).

## Summary

None of the three reliably carries points 7–12 as a whole. Each keeps part of them in its canon,
and each keeps a mechanism Deming rejected:

- **Lean** comes closest. Its canon, Toyota's statements and the best-known studies of Toyota,
  calls for leadership as teaching (7), stopping the line without fear (8) and workers improving
  their own jobs (12a). But it keeps fixed cycle times
  (11a), cascaded targets through hoshin kanri (11b) and individual evaluation (12b). Lean's own
  leaders say most adoptions are shallow: hoshin is often "little more than rebranded management by
  objectives". NUMMI shows it can deliver much of 7–12 under Toyota's management; studies of
  Western adoption find harm to autonomy and well-being in low-complexity work.
- **Six Sigma** fits Deming in its statistical core: common- versus special-cause variation puts
  defects on the system. The management programme around it, as GE and 3M ran it, contradicts 8,
  10, 11b and 12b directly: promotion and stock options gated on it, executive pay weighted to it,
  forced ranking with the bottom 10% removed every year, and green-belt quotas.
- **ISO 9000/9001** states much of 7–9 in its guidance on principles, including "take initiatives
  without fear", but certification audits against ISO 9001's requirements, which are silent on 8,
  11a and 12, and require measurable objectives at "functions, levels and processes" (11b). Deming
  called the standards "conformance specifications", "not enough". No study measures whether
  certification produces any of points 7–12.

A reading of the pattern, not a finding: the parts of 7–12 that cost management nothing to state
survive in the canon, and the parts that would remove management's own instruments (targets,
appraisal, ranking) do not. Practice evidence is thin everywhere, and absent for fear and pride of
workmanship.

## The points

From the Deming Institute's condensation of *Out of the Crisis*, pp. 23–24
(<https://deming.org/explore/fourteen-points/>):

7. Institute leadership: supervision helps people do a better job.
8. Drive out fear.
9. Break down barriers between departments.
10. Eliminate slogans, exhortations and targets for the workforce.
11. (a) Eliminate work standards (quotas); (b) eliminate management by objective and numerical
    goals. Substitute leadership.
12. Remove barriers to pride of workmanship: (a) for hourly workers; (b) for management and
    engineering, including abolishing the annual or merit rating and management by objective.

## Lean and the Toyota Production System

| Point | Canon | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Calls for: supervisors "as teachers" (Spear & Bowen) | NUMMI improved on GM's supervision; GM did not spread it |
| 8 | Calls for: andon, any operator can stop the line (Toyota) | Mixed: NUMMI workers still reported "fear tactics"; a 2017 Toyota suicide was ruled work-related harassment |
| 9 | Calls for: hoshin aligns "across departments" (LEI) | Thin |
| 10 | Silent | No evidence found |
| 11a | Contradicts in form: fixed cycle times; reframed as hypotheses to improve, not quotas | Contested: NUMMI workers set their own standards; a review finds harm in low-complexity manual work |
| 11b | Contradicts in mechanism: hoshin cascades numerical targets; its advocates distinguish it from MBO | Liker and Shook: most efforts are "little more than rebranded management by objectives (MBO)" |
| 12a | Calls for: frontline workers improve their own jobs | NUMMI satisfaction rose; Parker 2003 found lower autonomy on lean lines (unchecked) |
| 12b | Contradicts: Toyota evaluates individuals | 2019: Toyota's union considered a five-grade evaluation for raises, which "reflects management's wishes" |

## Six Sigma

| Point | Canon | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Partly: ASQ's syllabus covers "coaching multiple levels of leadership", meaning deploying projects | GE's "A" leader has "edge": "the courage to make the tough calls" |
| 8 | Silent in method; GE 1997: "unthinkable to hire into the Company, promote or tolerate those who cannot, or will not, commit to this way of work" | GE removed its bottom 10% every year; 3M paired it with 8,000 layoffs and an intensified performance review |
| 9 | Calls for cross-functional teams | GE's "boundaryless" idea predates Six Sigma; no study isolates Six Sigma's effect |
| 10 | Contradicts: Motorola's 3.4 defects-per-million target (checked only against Wikipedia) and GE's goal to "raise the bar" | Deming's *The New Economics* quotes Wheeler: "Zero Defects, and Six Sigma … all miss the point" (agent-checked) |
| 11a | Silent | 3M: people dreamed up green-belt programmes "to fill their quota" |
| 11b | Contradicts: ASQ's syllabus teaches SMART goals, OKRs and "a line of sight from performance measures to organizational strategies" | GE: executive pay "heavily weighted toward Six Sigma commitment and success" |
| 12a | Mostly silent; the variation model puts defects on the system | Two employee surveys found mild positives (abstracts only) |
| 12b | Contradicts: training "an ironclad prerequisite for promotion … and a requirement for any award of stock options" (GE 1997) | 3M researchers filled a "red book" of charts; Art Fry: "how fast a culture can be torn apart" |

The method and the programme separate cleanly in principle and rarely in practice. GE linked them
outright: its top 20% early-career performers were offered "a two-to-three-year Black Belt
assignment". The Deming Institute's writers deny the core is safe either: they argue a
specification-based focus is integral to Six Sigma (agent-checked via the Wayback Machine).

Key sources:

- GE annual reports, 1997 and 2000 (Welch's letters),
  <https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/NYSE_GE_1997.pdf> and
  <https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReportArchive/g/NYSE_GE_2000.pdf>. The 2000
  letter: GE "must remove that lower 10%, and keep removing it every year".
- Hindo, "At 3M, a struggle between efficiency and creativity", *BusinessWeek*, 11 June 2007,
  <https://effectuation.org/hubfs/Journal%20Articles/2016/06/3m-struggle-between-efficiency-and-creativity.pdf>.
  It also reports cuts to capital spending, which confound any causal claim.
- ASQ, Six Sigma Black Belt Body of Knowledge, 2022,
  <https://www.asq.org/cert/resource/pdf/certification/2022-SSBB-BoK.pdf>. Nothing on fear,
  merit rating or pride of workmanship.

## ISO 9000 and ISO 9001

| Point | Canon | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Calls for, in the principles; ISO 9001 5.1.1 requires "engaging, directing and supporting persons" only toward the quality management system | None found |
| 8 | Principles: "Empower people to determine constraints to performance and to take initiatives without fear"; requirements silent | None found |
| 9 | Principles: "reduced cross-functional barriers", "Promote collaboration"; requirements weak | None found |
| 10 | Contested: everyone must be aware of the quality policy and objectives (7.3), which critics read as a slogan | None found |
| 11a | Silent | None found |
| 11b | Contradicts, contested: objectives must "be measurable" at "relevant functions, levels and processes" (6.2); the counter-reading is that they are for processes, not people | None measured |
| 12a | Silent: "Recognize and acknowledge people's contribution" | None found |
| 12b | Leans against: principles suggest "self-evaluation of performance against personal objectives" | None found |

Practice evidence is indirect. Levine & Toffel (*Management Science*, 2010) found adopters had
lower death rates and higher growth in sales, employment and earnings, but measured none of
Deming's points, and say non-experimental data "cannot definitively attribute" the outcomes to
adoption. Boiral (*Organization Science*, 2003) found support for ISO 9000 "often … only
superficial", with "ceremonial integrators" among the responses (abstract only).

Key sources:

- ISO, *Quality management principles*, 2015,
  <https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/store/en/PUB100080.pdf>. Under "Engagement
  of people" it says "take initiatives without fear" and "self-evaluation of performance against
  personal objectives"; under "Process approach", "reduced cross-functional barriers".
- Deming, interviewed by Tim Stevens, *IndustryWeek*, 17 January 1994: "ISO 9000, 9001, 9002 are
  conformance specifications … Of course we must conform to requirements. But that's not enough;
  that won't do it",
  <https://www.industryweek.com/operations/quality/article/21963887/dr-deming-management-today-does-not-know-what-its-job-is-part-3>.
- Christopher Paris, "How does ISO 9001:2015 stack up against Deming's 14 points?", Oxebridge,
  2015, <https://www.oxebridge.com/emma/how-does-iso-90012015-stack-up-against-demings-14-points/>.
  A critic of ISO, so partisan.
- ISO 9001:2015 itself is paywalled; its clause wording here comes from secondary quotations.

## What would settle it

- **Studies that measure the points directly.** None was found for any of the three: fear or
  psychological safety, use of individual targets, appraisal practice, pride in work, compared
  between adopters and non-adopters. This is the central gap.
- **Method separated from programme.** Six Sigma deployments with and without forced ranking or
  bonus links, compared. The same for hoshin kanri used for direction versus for appraisal.
- **Toyota's own appraisal of managers and engineers,** and whether the 2019 five-grade proposal
  was adopted.
- **Primary texts not yet read:** Seddon's *In Pursuit of Quality: The Case Against ISO 9000*
  (1997), Harry & Schroeder's *Six Sigma* (2000), Parker 2003 in full, the ISO 9001:2015 standard.

## How this was checked

Three research agents, one per methodology, gathered sources and marked each claim checked or
unchecked. These claims were then re-checked against the source by the author of this note:
ISO's principles document (all three quoted phrases); Deming's 1994 *IndustryWeek* quote; Liker
and Shook on MBO; Smalley on the cascade and appraisal; the *Globe and Mail* five-grade report;
Womack; GE's 1997 and 2000 letters (every quote above); the 3M article (every quote above); and
ASQ's syllabus (SMART goals, OKRs, line of sight, coaching, and the absence of "fear"). Claims
marked "agent-checked" were read by an agent but not re-checked; claims marked "unchecked" were
not read in full by anyone. Everything else in the tables comes from the agents' reports, checked
by them against the cited source.
